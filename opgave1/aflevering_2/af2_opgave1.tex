\section{Exercise 1}
\textit{Let $F$ be a vector field on $\mathbb{R}^3$ given by:}
\[F(x,y,z)=(xy^2z,x^2yz,\frac{1}{2}x^2y^2)\]
\textit{calculate the integral:}
\[\oint_C \mathbb{F} \cdot dr\]
\textit{where $C=\{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 | x^2 + y^2 = 1, z = 4\}$ (for positive orientation in the xy-plane)}

%example 2.10 kan bruges her

Because x and y is defined as a unit circle in C, with a constant Z value, this can be
rewritte as using polar coordinates, which becomes
\[x = cos(\theta),
\qquad 
y = sin(\theta),
\qquad z = 4
\]
This changes the bound of integration to:
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\]

\[r(\theta) = 
\begin{pmatrix}
cos(\theta) \\ sin(\theta) \\ 4
\end{pmatrix}
\]
And F can also be rewritte in terms of parameter $\theta$ as $\textbf{dr}$, where:
\[dr(\theta) = 
\begin{pmatrix}
-sin(\theta) \\ cos(\theta) \\ 0
\end{pmatrix}\]
which makes $F(dr(\theta))$
\[
F(dr(\theta)) = 
\begin{pmatrix}
    4cos(\theta)sin^2(\theta) \\
    4sin(\theta)cos(\theta) \\
    \frac{1}{2}cos^2(\theta)sin^2(\theta)
\end{pmatrix}
\]
This makes the line integral
\[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{F} \cdot dr 
\qquad 
=
\qquad 
\begin{pmatrix}
    4cos(\theta)sin^2(\theta) \\
    4sin(\theta)cos(\theta) \\
    \frac{1}{2}cos^2(\theta)sin^2(\theta)
\end{pmatrix}
\cdot 
\begin{pmatrix}
-sin(\theta) \\ cos(\theta) \\ 0
\end{pmatrix}
\]
Yielding two terms
\[\int_{0}^{2\pi} -(4cos(\theta)sin^3(\theta)) + (4cos^3(\theta)sin(\theta))d\theta\]
This will be split up into two integrals and then solved\\
First term
\[\int -(4cos(\theta)sin^3(\theta))d\theta\]
\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}sin(\theta) = cos(\theta),
\qquad
u = sin(\theta),
\qquad
\frac{du}{d\theta} = cos(\theta),
\qquad
du = cos(\theta)
\]
\[-4\int cos(\theta)d\theta sin^3(\theta) \Rightarrow -4\int u^3 du \Rightarrow -4 \frac{u^4}{4} + C = -sin^4(\theta) + C\]
Second Term
\[\int (4cos^3(\theta)sin(\theta))d\theta\]
\[
\frac{d}{d\theta} cos(\theta) = -sin(\theta),
\qquad
u = cos(\theta),
\qquad
du = -sin(\theta),
\qquad
sin(\theta)d\theta = -du
\]
\[4\int cos^3(\theta)sin(\theta)d\theta\ \Rightarrow 4\int u^3 (-du) \Rightarrow -4\frac{u^4}{4}+C = -cos^4(\theta) + C\]
\[\left[-sin^4(\theta)-cos^4(\theta) \right]^{2\pi}_0 \Rightarrow (-sin^4(2\pi)-cos^4(2\pi)) - (-sin^4(0)-cos^4(0)) = (-0^4 - 1^4)-(-0^4-1^4) = (-1)-(-1) = 0\]
